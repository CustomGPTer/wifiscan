Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026. This policy covers the WiFi Scan app for Android (package uk.jj.wifiscan) and this website. WiFi Scan is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Everything WiFi Scan keeps stays on your phone. Nothing is sent to the developer or to anyone else.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • WiFi Scan goes online only for the tests and tools you run, a check of your public IP address and provider, and scheduled tests if you switch them on.

What stays on your phone

WiFi Scan keeps these in the app's private storage:

  • Your speed test history, saved places, heatmaps and floor-plan pictures.
  • Devices seen on your WiFi, with any labels you give them.
  • The monitoring log and your settings.

Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for WiFi Scan.

Location and camera

Android requires location permission before any app can see WiFi names. WiFi Scan never reads your position, stores it or sends it anywhere. The camera is used only during a camera walk, to track where you are; no picture is saved or sent. Camera walks use Google Play Services for AR on your phone.

Backup files

A backup is made only when you ask for one, or every week if you switch that on. The file is locked with a password you choose (PBKDF2 and AES-256) and saved where you pick, such as Google Drive. Without the password nobody can open it, including Google and the developer.

Keeping and deleting your data

History, devices, places, heatmaps and the log can each be cleared in the app, and uninstalling WiFi Scan deletes everything it has kept.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands on your phone.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies and no analytics. The only script on it unlocks the download once the lawful-use box is ticked. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, under its own privacy statement.
